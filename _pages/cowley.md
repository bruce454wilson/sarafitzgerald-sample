# Anne Pyne Crowley Interview

![](https://img1.wsimg.com/isteam/ip/cf3b878d-146c-4bd2-864f-413d644dca05/FinalJacketDownloaded.jpg/:/rs=w:1200,m)

Anne Pyne Cowley responded to the author’s questions in email messages dated May 9, 2019 and July 12, 2019.

May 9, 2019

Things were mostly fine when I was a grad student at Michigan, or I may have been too busy to see problems. In those days people, especially women, weren't interviewed for jobs—one’s supervisor just called up friends and suddenly you had a position. My then-husband got a professorship at the University of Chicago that way, so when we moved there I had no position.

One of the faculty members hired me as a postdoc, but hardly paid me anything. When I told him he was paying more to his grad students, he told me that I had a husband to support me so I only needed money for “perfume and furs” (neither of which I have ever wanted/used). After several years, my husband and I were both offered faculty positions back at Michigan, but we were told that for financial reasons my appointment would be delay[ed] for a year. Of course, that appointment never came and I continued to work using grants from NSF [the National Science Foundation], although U-M set my salary and had me teach.

I have lots of memories of things men in the department said/did to me. When I was pregnant with my second child I was told, like I couldn't have an office [because] the previous director wouldn't allow it. My husband gave me his office and started to work from home. Then suddenly a space was found for me.

Lots of similar things happened and I finally went to court (painful experience) and although they found in my favor no job was given to me until I was

offered a full professorship at ASU. I talked to the U-M dean [of the College of Literature, Science and the Arts] but got nowhere, so I decided that it was time to get out of that hostile atmosphere.

The whole experience there was awful and cost me my marriage.

**E-mailed responses to questions, dated July 12, 2019.**

**Q:** At the time of the HEW complaint, were both you and your husband working for the Astronomy Department? I noticed that HEW pointed to the discrepancy in the salaries that were paid to Margo and Hugh Aller in that department, and wondered if you were contemporaries and whether your situation was different?

**A:** I am unsure if I knew about [the HEW complaint] and it certainly didn't help me in any way at U-M.

I was at Michigan with Margo and Hugh.. Hugh's father was a prof in astronomy so of course Hugh was already there. Margo is still active and shares a room for retirees with my ex (Charles Cowley). All of us women were terribly underpaid back then - extremely annoying!

I gave up on U-M when, after many years of putting off giving me a faculty position (they loved that I was able to bring in NSF grants and that they could set my pay even though they in no way helped me earning those grants), I was offered a full professor position at Arizona State. So I went in to talk to the Michigan dean. He didn't care and told me that Michigan was a much better school than ASU. However, they did then appoint me to the U-M faculty, but I decided to leave anyway.

**Q:** In your department, were there unrealistic expectations regarding things that faculty wives were expected to do, (e.g. serve as hostesses for their husbands for departmental dinner parties?) I've heard this complaint from several of your contemporaries, but the culture of each department may be different.

While I was at Michigan they never tried to get me to host dinners or anything like that. After my husband and I got our PhDs at U-M, he was appointed to the faculty at the University of Chicago and we moved to Williams Bay, Wisconsin, where the astronomers worked (Yerkes Observatory). There, wives were expected to "pour tea and bake cookies" for the weekly colloquia. I refused saying I didn't have time. That didn't charm anyone in charge, but I did get away without doing it. Here at ASU, I was never expected to do anything social and only rarely joined in any social events.

All this happened a long time ago, but I confess to still being very annoyed about how I was treated at Michigan. But I also had some very awkward moments at professional meetings—the then-president of the AAS sexually assaulted me in an elevator at a meeting in Italy. I never dared to complain since he had a huge amount of power over me and could have ended my career. That old fart died a few years ago -yeah!
